02

Turn the percentage into a model.

  1. Start with turnover, not a deposit

    If 100 units are staked, 60 are returned and those 60 are staked again, turnover is 160 units. The calculator uses the amount exposed to the game each time.

  2. Calculate the theoretical return

    Expected gross return = turnover × RTP / 100. Expected loss before fees = turnover × (1 − RTP / 100). This assumes the entered RTP applies to all included stakes.

  3. Add the known fixed fee

    Total expected cost = expected loss + fixed fee. Compare the resulting costs in the same unit. Any conditional charge or reward needs its own model.

1 pp

A small percentage can have a visible cost.

For hypothetical turnover of 10,000 units, moving from 96% to 97% RTP reduces theoretical loss by 100 units. If the second scenario adds a fixed 50-unit fee, its expected cost is 350 rather than 400.

10,000 × (1 − 0.96) = 400
10,000 × (1 − 0.97) + 50 = 350

The 1 percentage point change is a difference in the theoretical rate. It is not a 1% increase in the chance of winning.

04

Questions, answered

Is theoretical loss a prediction of my session?

No. It is an expectation under the RTP and turnover assumptions you enter. Individual outcomes can vary widely; the tool does not calculate a session win probability or a likely payout range.

Should I enter my deposit or my total stakes?

Enter total turnover: the sum of every stake. Re-staking a payout increases turnover even when you have not deposited more money. A deposit is not a substitute for this input.

Does 96% RTP mean I get 96% of a deposit back?

No. RTP describes a long-run relationship between stakes and gross prizes under a game’s rules. It is not a promise about a particular deposit or session.

Can fees change which scenario has the lower expected cost?

Yes. A higher RTP can be offset by higher fixed fees. Compare the two total expected costs using the same turnover and unit; fees are charged once per scenario in this model.

Are bonuses, cashback or taxes included?

No. Conditions, caps, eligibility and the treatment of losses make those more complicated than one flat adjustment. This tool only models theoretical gross return and a known fixed fee.

What is saved or sent?
